Quick start

Launch with the desktop shortcut or by typing jobhunt in a terminal. The app opens at 127.0.0.1:8765; if it's already running, launching again just opens the existing window.

  1. Refresh sources (masthead button) — pulls fresh jobs from every source. Do this daily; it takes a minute or two.
  2. Upload your CV on the CV page — every job gets a match %, and the default sort starts favoring jobs that fit you.
  3. Set your location on Settings (e.g. Egypt) — jobs you can't actually get (like "US only" remote roles) sink in the ranking.
  4. Search on Jobs, queue interesting ones on Apply, and use Tailor before you send anything.
The daily loop: Refresh → check Jobs with your saved filters → queue → tailor → apply. Alerts (below) can replace the "check" step entirely.

How ranking works

The default relevance sort is: quality score × (1 + 1.5 × CV match) × reachability. Quality score is a transparent 0–4 (salary listed, recent, detailed description…). CV match kicks in once a CV is uploaded. Reachability demotes geo-restricted jobs when your location is set — a "US only" role drops to roughly a third of its weight. A small pill above the results discloses when this reordering is active. Sort by newest instead when you want pure recency.

Career stage vs Job type: "Internship" lives under Job type (it's a contract kind). Career stage is about seniority. They're separate on purpose — filtering Career stage = Entry does not hide internships.

Local Jobs

/local — zero-experience work near you

Built for job-seekers who need work, not a career ladder: cleaning, warehouse, retail, kitchen, delivery, reception, customer service, care, security. Defaults to non-tech; the What kind of work toggle switches to tech. Sorted newest-first, because local hiring moves fast.

  • UK — via Reed (free API key needed, Settings page has a "Get a free key" link).
  • Germany — via Arbeitsagentur, no key needed (10,000+ jobs).
  • Egypt — region dropdown includes Cairo, Giza, Alexandria (Arabic names work too).

Apply

/apply — where applying actually happens

Every job's apply link is classified into one of three flows, shown as a colored badge. This tells you what you're walking into before you click:

  • ATS form — a known application system (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby…). Usually a clean form you can fill directly. The easiest wins.
  • Job board — the application happens on a board like Wuzzuf or Bayt, and you may need an account there. Not one click away, but predictable.
  • Company site — the company's own careers page. Every one is different; this is the bucket the assistant handoff exists for.

Tabs filter by flow, with live counts. Results use the same blended relevance as the Jobs page, so your best-fit, most-reachable options surface first.

The application queue & the Cowork handoff

/apply?view=queue — only reachable from this computer

Press Queue on any job to start tracking it as an application. The queue enforces two human decision points — nothing is ever submitted on your behalf without both:

queued→ drafted→ you review the exact fields→ approved→ submitted

An assistant (like Claude Cowork) can pick up queued applications through a local-only API, draft the form answers from your Profile, and report back what it would fill. You see that draft field-by-field and press Approve — or send it back. Rejected or failed applications can be re-queued.

To turn the handoff on: add JOBHUNT_COWORK_EXPORT=1 to your configuration (see Configuration), restart, then run jobhunt apply export to see the handoff document. Safety is structural: job descriptions are wrapped as untrusted data so a malicious posting can't instruct the assistant, the field list is fixed by jobhunt (an assistant can never invent new personal fields), and submissions are hard-stopped outside an exact-host domain allow-list.

Tailor a CV to a job

/apply/tailor/<job> — Tailor link on any apply card, or jobhunt tailor <id>

A one-page prep sheet for a specific job:

  • Keyword gap — which of the job's keywords your CV already covers, and which are missing. Works for non-tech roles too (forklift, barista, care work — not just Python).
  • Suggested Skills line — your matched keywords first, since application systems scan for them.
  • ATS readiness check — flags the formatting that makes application systems mis-read a CV: missing contact info, non-standard section headers, multi-column or table layouts, garbled encoding, unreasonable length.

Everything is deterministic keyword analysis — it never invents a skill or an achievement. Download the sheet as Markdown with the .md button.

Profile

/profile — only reachable from this computer

The details application forms always ask for — name, email, phone, links, work authorization, notice period — entered once, reused by the queue and the assistant handoff.

Privacy is enforced by design, not policy: the page refuses connections from other devices even if the app is exposed on your network; there are deliberately no passport or national-ID fields; and the form rejects anything that looks like a pasted API key or password, so a credential can never end up in the store. The data lives in your local database and appears in exactly one place outside it: the handoff document you explicitly enable.

CV Match

/cv

Upload a PDF CV and every job gets a match percentage. With the optional ML extra installed the match is semantic (meaning-based); without it, a keyword overlap score — the upload always works either way, and the page tells you which mode is active.

The match % is a pure "how well do I fit this role" number — location and reachability never contaminate it. Ranking applies those separately, so the badge always means what it says.

Alerts

/alerts

Save any search and get notified when new jobs match it. Three channels, each optional:

ChannelTurn it on
DesktopOn by default while the app runs
TelegramSet JOBHUNT_TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN + JOBHUNT_TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID (create a bot with @BotFather)
EmailSet JOBHUNT_SMTP_HOST + JOBHUNT_SMTP_TO (+ user/password if your server needs them)

Two options per saved search worth knowing:

  • Source filter — watch only chosen sources (e.g. just Wuzzuf + JSearch).
  • Priority — a priority search gets its own fast-poll cycle (JOBHUNT_FAST_POLL_MINUTES), re-scraping just its sources on a tight interval. Being among the first applicants is a real edge; this is how you get it.

Sources

/sources

All configured sources, grouped by type. Add your own — a company's Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby/Workday board, a Jooble or JSearch search — and it survives app updates (your entries are stored separately from the built-in list).

  • Company boards: the board slug is the company name in its careers URL — stripe from boards.greenhouse.io/stripe.
  • JSearch / Careerjet searches: the "board" is a query — developer|Egypt, python developer|remote. Each JSearch search costs one API request per pass (free tier ≈ 200/month) — a built-in 20-hour cooldown holds jsearch to about one sweep a day no matter how often refreshes run.
  • Reddit hiring threads: the board is a subreddit name (forhire, jobbit) — only [Hiring]-marked posts come through, feeding the freelance lane.

Broken sources take care of themselves: after 3 consecutive failed scrapes a source is skipped automatically, retried once a day, and re-enabled the moment it works again. The Settings page shows a health summary; jobhunt doctor gives the full report.

Settings

/settings

  • API keys — Jooble (free), Reed (free, unlocks UK local jobs), JSearch (free RapidAPI tier, unlocks indirect Egypt + remote coverage), Careerjet (free affiliate ID, a real Egypt index). Keys are masked on screen, excluded from backups, and the Test keys button verifies Jooble/Reed with a real one-job request.
  • Your location — one line (e.g. Egypt) that powers reachability ranking.
  • Source health — offline / needs-attention counts at a glance.
  • Updates — Check tells you if a newer version exists; Install actually upgrades. Uninstall is one click and removes the app cleanly.

Egypt & Arabic

jobhunt treats Egypt as a first-class market, not an afterthought:

  • Wuzzuf — the largest Egyptian job board, ~2,900 jobs via its public feed, with structured career-level data mapped directly (no guessing).
  • JSearch — Google-for-Jobs aggregation reaching postings on Bayt, LinkedIn and company sites that can't be scraped directly. Bring-your-own-key, off by default.
  • MENA employers — Careem, Tamara, Thndr, Bayut|dubizzle and other regional companies via their public boards.
  • Careerjet — a public search API with a dedicated Egypt index (careerjet.com.eg); free affiliate ID on the Settings page.
  • Arabic extraction — level, employment type and experience are detected from Arabic text, Arabic-Indic digits are understood, and when an Arabic posting gives no signal the job is honestly labeled "unknown" instead of guessed wrong.
  • RTL rendering — Arabic titles and companies display right-to-left correctly.
  • Remote reality check — the eligibility filter plus ranking demotion means "remote (US only)" jobs stop wasting your time.
  • Salary context — EGP and GCC currency estimates instead of US-centric numbers.

Troubleshooting

  • A country/source shows no jobs — run jobhunt doctor; it names exactly which sources fail and why. Key-gated sources (Reed, Jooble, JSearch) report "no key" until one is set.
  • CV upload errors — the page explains the cause (empty PDF, image-only scan…). Export a text-based PDF and re-upload.
  • Styles look stale after an update — hard-refresh once (Ctrl+F5); normally cache-busting handles this automatically.
  • Geo/apply badges missing on old jobs — they fill in as jobs are re-seen; run a Refresh.
  • JSearch stopped returning jobs — check quota on your RapidAPI dashboard; the free tier resets monthly. The app retries rate-limits automatically and spaces its requests.
  • Something else — the in-app Help page covers every filter and error; data_dir/jobhunt.log has details for bug reports.
